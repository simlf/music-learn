# Sketch 012: Interactive Theory

## Design Question
The /theory page is currently static reading. A returning classical pianist with beginner
music theory needs interactive lessons that build understanding and hand off cleanly into
their first drill. How should an interactive beginner theory lesson look and flow, and how
does it lead into a drill? Worked example across all three variants: **the major third vs
the minor third** (the interval that makes a chord happy vs sad), since thirds are the core
of chord quality.

## How to View

## Variants
- **A · Guided steps** — the lesson is a vertical sequence of small steps. Each step pairs
  one plain-English sentence with one DO-action on a mini keyboard (tap C, then the note
  4 keys up, then "hear it"). Right = verdigris light + check; wrong = gentle retry with a
  count hint. Steps lock green as you go and the next opens. Ends on a "Try this in a drill →"
  handoff card.
- **B · Lesson map + focused card** — a letterpress curriculum index (Intervals → Triads →
  7th chords) of lesson cards with done / current / locked marks. Tapping the current lesson
  opens it in place as one focused interactive lesson (major then minor third on the mini
  keyboard, with a check). Completing it marks the card done, unlocks the Triads unit, and
  reveals the drill handoff.
- **C · Play-first discovery** — minimal instruction. Start at the keyboard: "Play C. Now
  play the note 4 keys to the right." Theory emerges from doing: acting reveals short inline
  nuggets ("That's a MAJOR THIRD — bright, happy"), then "play only 3 keys up" reveals the
  minor third by contrast. Ends in the same drill handoff.

## What to Look For
- Does the plain-English + one-action pairing feel achievable for a true beginner, or does
  it move too slowly for a returning pianist who reads music?
- Where does the "aha" of major-vs-minor land hardest: told-then-do (A/B) or do-then-named (C)?
- Is the drill handoff a natural next step or a jarring context switch? Compare the three
  handoff framings (steps recap / unlock + reps / two-moods).
- Mini keyboard: are taps, correct lights (interval colors), and the gentle wrong-retry
  legible on a phone? Count hints on wrong taps — helpful or noisy?
- Map vs linear: does B's curriculum index give useful orientation, or does A's bare stack
  feel lighter and less intimidating?
