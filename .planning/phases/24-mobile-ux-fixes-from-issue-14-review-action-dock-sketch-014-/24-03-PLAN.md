<objective>
Fix issue #14 point 4: a beginner typed AM7 (meaning A major 7) but the app returned A-C-E-G and he expected C♯ because "A major has C♯ in its key signature". Two gaps: (1) casing/symbol ambiguity between Am7 (minor) and Amaj7 (major), and (2) no in-place explanation that lowercase `m` means a minor third derived from the root's interval formula, independent of any key signature.

Implement sketch 016 as composed by the locked decision: B's disambiguation chips at INPUT time (shown only for genuinely ambiguous casing), A's expandable "Pourquoi ces notes ?" panel on EVERY recognized result, with C's minor-vs-major side-by-side duo folded into A's panel as the deepest content (same dossier-in-deepest-rung pattern as sketch 010's hint ladder).

Purpose: Turn a confusing "wrong-looking" lookup into a teaching moment, closing the beginner comprehension gap that the single-user builder actually hit.
Output: A pure casing-ambiguity helper, a WhyTheseNotes teaching-panel component (with the folded duo), Lookup-page wiring for both the disambiguation prompt and the always-available why-panel, bilingual i18n keys, and tests at each layer.
</objective>
