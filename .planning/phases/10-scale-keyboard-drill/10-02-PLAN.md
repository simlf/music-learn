<objective>
Build the `ScaleKeyboardDrill.svelte` component and wire it into the /scales Practice tab, replacing the retired degree-ladder surface (`ScalePractice.svelte` + `scaleDrill.ts`) with a tonic-anchored 2-octave keyboard drill graded by pitch class. Delete the retired files and their tests so no letter-equality grading remains (SCALE-04, SCALE-05).

Purpose: fix the confusing degree ladder — the user practices a scale by playing it up then down on a real keyboard where the tonic is the leftmost highlighted key and the run ends on a distinct octave. The Explore surface stays untouched.
Output: the new component + test, the rewired page, and the removal of the old surface.

Design decision recorded (discretionary point resolved): the drill renders its OWN 2-octave keyboard using the shared `keyboardLayout` geometry rather than delegating fills to `PianoKeyboard`'s `notes` path. Rationale: `assignNotesToKeys` advances its cursor past the returning octave when the leading tone is a black key (e.g. G major's F♯ before the octave G), collapsing degree 8 onto the tonic key — so it cannot fill a distinct octave key, and its `wrongLetters` prop paints a ✕ + batch stamp, which is the wrong semantic for the momentary gray wrong-flash. Rendering our own keys (reusing the pure geometry and the global interval-color tokens) gives correct octave placement and a clean transient flash without touching the chord-critical shared PianoKeyboard.
</objective>
