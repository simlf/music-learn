<objective>
Make the improvisation guide's greyed notes explain themselves, and make the explanation audible.

Purpose: the captain used the shipped guide and could not tell WHY a greyed note is to be avoided, which is the one thing he says matters most ("tant que je connais les notes à ne pas jouer"). PR #74 shipped a single generic line ("a half step above E: held on a strong beat it rubs"), which states the rule without teaching the reason, and greyed still reads as forbidden. He also asked for an audible part, and the app already owns a chord-playback engine.

Output: per-avoid-note copy naming the colliding chord tone, its role in the chord and the minor 9th they make; the passing-note nuance and the "same note, other chord" escape where the engine can name one; a visual and verbal split between colour and careful; a first-class blue-note row; and chord-plus-note playback on the keyboard and on each note row, cancellable, through the existing audio engine.
</objective>

<decisions>
- D-01: the avoid explanation is written per COLLIDING DEGREE, not per tension label. The engine already derives every careful tag from one rule (half step above a chord tone); it now also reports WHICH tone, so the copy can be specific without a lookup table of chord/note pairs.
- D-02: the A/B comparison is chord tone then the half step above it (E then F over Cmaj7), not two arbitrary scale notes. It is the shortest demonstration of the exact claim the copy makes.
- D-03: the keyboard-tap path plays the chord only. PianoKeyboard's inspect mode already sounds the tapped key, and duplicating it there would double the note.
- D-04: restraint on controls (the captain has complained elsewhere about apps with too many controls): one button per explained note, no global player, no volume, no tempo, no repeat.
- D-05: the blue note gets a row in the body because "why does B♭ work over C" was his earlier question, and an answer folded three levels deep is not an answer.
</decisions>
